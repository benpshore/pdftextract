Harbour sensors cut night-time fuel use by a third

By Mira Okonkwo and Tom Ashdown · 14 March 2026

AD_SLOT_TEXT Sponsored: buy a boat.

The east quay pilot replaced the harbour's timed bilge pumps with level sensors last winter, and the first full season of readings shows the pumps ran for a third less time after dark. The harbour master's office published the figures on Friday, together with the meter logs that the volunteer group collected each morning.

Each sensor sits in a short tube bolted to the pontoon frame, and it only wakes the pump when water reaches a marked line. The old timers switched the pumps on for ten minutes every hour whether the bilge was wet or dry, which is where most of the overnight fuel went.

What the logs show

  • Pump run time fell from 240 minutes a night to 158 minutes on average.
  • Fuel deliveries to the quay generator dropped from four to three per month.
  • No pontoon flooded during the trial, including the two storm weeks in January.

We expected a small saving and got a large one, mostly because the timers were doing so much pointless work.

Next steps for the west quay

  1. Fit sensors to the remaining twelve pontoons before autumn.
  2. Publish the meter logs monthly instead of at the end of the season.
