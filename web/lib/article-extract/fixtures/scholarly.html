Seasonal drift in pontoon sensor calibration

Mira Okonkwo, Tom Ashdown, Beatriz Ríos · DOI: 10.9999/jhi.2026.0042 · PubMed · PMC

Abstract

We measured drift in twelve level sensors over one winter. Each sensor was checked against a reference gauge every fortnight, and the offsets were recorded in the harbour log together with the water temperature and the salinity reading of the day.

Methods

The sensors were mounted in identical tubes on pontoons B through M. A reference gauge was read by two observers at low water, and the mean of the two readings was taken as the true level for the comparison. Offsets larger than five millimetres were logged as calibration events.

Results

Drift grew slowly through December and then stabilised once the water temperature stopped falling. The largest offsets appeared on the pontoons nearest the river mouth, where salinity changed most between tides, which suggests that the sensing element is sensitive to conductivity rather than to temperature alone.
